# Design Research

Live audit completed **2026-08-16**. References were selected for AI research, research engineering, machine-learning engineering, and technical project communication—not for general design-showcase aesthetics.

## Current Site Audit

Sources: [homepage](https://rogerbaiges.github.io/), [research](https://rogerbaiges.github.io/research), [projects](https://rogerbaiges.github.io/projects)

### What already works

- A clear portrait, direct CV and project links, and dedicated research, projects, awards, CV, and contact routes make the content inspectable.
- The sequence from introduction to projects and recognition is understandable.
- The research route's Questions / Methods / Early Evidence structure is a useful foundation for deeper project storytelling.
- Semantic structure, keyboard focus, reduced-motion handling, and responsive navigation provide a sound accessibility baseline.

### What weakens Roger's positioning

- The hero still leads with “AI student at UPC,” `9.10/10`, and “building toward graduate study.” It omits incoming EPFL study, Top 5%, four AI hackathon wins, and the strongest CVC and Serimag results.
- CVC is framed mainly as a bachelor's thesis and Serimag as work Roger “helped” with. This understates ownership and outcomes.
- “Projects that show how I learn” reinforces a student interpretation.
- Selected work is presented as interchangeable text cards with tags and repository links. No process, system diagram, technical challenge, or result is visible at a glance.
- Repeated rounded cards, pills, light borders, and monospace labels resemble a configurable template rather than a distinctive personal identity.
- Six navigation links, multiple hero actions, four social pills, and a theme control compete for attention in the first viewport.
- The footer exposes the customized Lumina base instead of ending on Roger's identity and contact intent.

## Maria Benchmark

Sources: [Maria Pilligua's homepage](https://mpilligua.github.io/index.html), [project archive](https://mpilligua.github.io/projects.html), [HyperNVD](https://hypernvd.github.io/), [LayeredDoc](https://layereddoc.github.io/)

Useful principles:

- The opening combines current institution and company, research territory, publication count, and first-author count; an inline highlight strip immediately reinforces the most selective evidence.
- Each featured project pairs real visual evidence with title, authorship, venue, a concise explanation, and direct Page / Paper / Code links.
- Experience and education use recognizable organization marks and link back to the work completed there.
- Academic results and award selectivity are contextualized rather than presented as unsupported labels.
- Hackathon entries combine placement, problem, implementation, and an evidence link.
- On mobile, the portrait moves first and project rows become deliberate one-column stories.

Do not copy Maria's conventional academic-publication syntax, dense button clusters, emoji headings, or autoplay media. Roger needs comparable evidence density expressed through system diagrams, engineering results, and clearer progressive hierarchy.

## Curated Practitioner References

- [Chip Huyen](https://huyenchip.com/) — Opens with a plain-language professional thesis, then substantiates it with named roles, shipped work, company-building, teaching, and quantified publishing outcomes. **Principle:** state the identity simply, then prove it with verbs, artifacts, and results while retaining human voice.
- [Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/) — Organizes contribution around writing, speaking, prototyping, and teaching, with direct artifact links and quantified bodies of work. **Principle:** demonstrate range through inspectable output, not skill tags.
- [Shreya Shankar](https://www.sh-reya.com/) — States a clear research mission and grounds a flagship system with adoption evidence and concrete user groups. **Principle:** connect technical novelty to who uses it and why it matters.
- [Anish Athalye](https://anishathalye.com/) and [Semlib](https://anishathalye.com/semlib/) — The project story proceeds from an executable demonstration to origin problem, design, benefits, case study, result, and code. **Principle:** communicate problem, mechanism, and proof in that order.
- [Jay Alammar](https://jalammar.github.io/) — A focused technical-communication reference rather than a layout reference. **Principle:** labeled stages and restrained interaction can make abstract model behavior understandable without fabricating a product interface.

## Synthesis

- First viewport: identity and trajectory, one direct headline, a short explanation, three proof items, then Explore Work and CV actions.
- Homepage narrative: research depth → industry execution → ambitious systems → education and recognition → personal context and contact.
- Project-row formula: **problem → system flow → Roger's ownership → strongest bounded result → evidence link**.
- Use native HTML/CSS/SVG diagrams as the primary imagery for nonvisual technical work. Never invent dashboards, experiments, or product screens.
- Connect recognition to the work that earned it instead of isolating every award from its project.
- Let CVC prove research depth, Serimag prove real-world execution, and independent projects prove ambition and range.
- Use one designed identity. Motion should clarify sequence, state, or clickability and remain complete under reduced motion.
- Mobile should preserve the evidence hierarchy and simplify diagrams rather than merely compress desktop composition.
